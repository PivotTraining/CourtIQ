# CourtIQ product frontier — October 2026

CourtIQ's strongest opportunity is not to become another drill library or another manual stat tracker. The product should own a closed development loop:

**record what happened → explain why it matters → prescribe the work → verify whether the player improved.**

## Where basketball-development products are moving

### 1. Camera-first computer vision

The category is moving from manual-only tracking toward phone-camera measurement of shots, movement, form, and reps. HomeCourt has long used mobile computer vision for shot and movement tracking, while Ballogy has expanded its vision stack for shot detection and training analysis.

CourtIQ direction: keep manual Gametime reliable, then add camera assistance where it removes friction without sacrificing data confidence.

### 2. Automatic game-film intelligence

Hooper markets smartphone video that identifies players and game events, then generates stats, highlights, and shot-by-shot analytics. PlaySight and Hudl are pushing automated capture, tagging, reporting, and searchable game film.

CourtIQ direction: create a single game timeline where stats and video clips share the same event IDs. A made three should be able to open the exact clip. A turnover should do the same.

### 3. Adaptive training prescriptions

Ballogy, Level Up, and newer AI-training products increasingly move beyond libraries into individualized plans based on assessments, weaknesses, goals, and prior progress.

CourtIQ direction: recommendations should become prescriptions. If the evidence says left-side finishing is the current gap, CourtIQ should assign a specific workout, record completion, and compare later games.

### 4. Whole-athlete context

Ballogy's 2026 Move, Train, Recover direction combines skill work with strength/recovery context and AI rep tracking. This reflects a broader shift from isolated skill metrics to athlete readiness and workload.

CourtIQ direction: preserve the mental-performance journal and eventually connect optional recovery/readiness inputs to basketball outcomes. Avoid making medical or causal claims the data cannot support.

### 5. Coach and family operating systems

The category is increasingly pairing athlete apps with web dashboards for coaches, assignments, progress monitoring, roster management, and accountability.

CourtIQ direction: player, parent, coach, and assistant-coach roles should be real backend permissions. The coach product should center on roster development, assignments, game review, and progress evidence.

### 6. Recruiting and shareable evidence

Basketball platforms are increasingly turning verified development data into profiles, clips, reports, and recruiting artifacts.

CourtIQ direction: build a player-development profile that can combine verified game summaries, shooting maps, trend lines, selected clips, and training history. Label manually entered data clearly.

## CourtIQ's frontier sequence

1. Production-safe ownership, persistence, and offline/poor-Wi-Fi reliability.
2. Desktop analytics and reporting that are substantially better than the phone view.
3. Prescribed training tied directly to game evidence.
4. Event-linked video and automatic clip organization.
5. Camera-assisted stat and movement detection with confidence indicators and human correction.
6. Coach/team workflows with verified invitations and permissions.
7. Family visibility and youth-consent controls.
8. Subscription billing only after the paid workflows are complete and verified.
9. Recruiting/shareable player-development profiles.
10. Optional recovery/wearable integrations after the core basketball loop is strong.

## Product rule

CourtIQ should distinguish **recorded fact**, **calculated metric**, **model inference**, and **coaching recommendation** in the interface. The more AI the product adds, the more important that distinction becomes.
